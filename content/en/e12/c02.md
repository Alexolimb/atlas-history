The Second World War ended with yesterday's allies dividing Europe and almost immediately ceasing to trust one another. The **Cold War** ran from 1945 to 1991. In forty-six years the two main participants never once fought each other directly — and that is the whole point of it.

## A peace held together by fear

The reason was new in history: for the first time both sides had a weapon whose use left no winner. The logic was called mutually assured destruction, and it sounds insane, but it worked: attacking was pointless, because the answer arrived regardless.

It held by a thread more often than is usually remembered. In 1962, during the crisis over Cuba, the world came closer to an exchange of strikes than ever before or since. Several times warning systems raised a false alarm, and the decision not to start a war was taken by a particular duty officer with a few minutes and standing orders that said the opposite.

There was plenty of fighting all the same — by other hands and on other people's territory. Korea, Vietnam, Afghanistan, dozens of conflicts in Africa and Latin America. The war was "cold" only for those living in Moscow and Washington.

The side effects were enormous. The race put humans in space twelve years after the first satellite and on the Moon soon after. A network designed to survive a nuclear strike became the internet.

## Half the world got its own countries

At the same time something even larger by the number of people affected was under way. The European empires fell apart: within twenty years of the war, dozens of countries in Asia and Africa, home to roughly a third of humanity, became independent.

The road differed. **Mahatma Gandhi**, killed in 1948, secured the British departure from India without once calling for armed struggle — and showed that this was possible. In Algeria and Vietnam the same thing was paid for with years of war. **Nelson Mandela** spent twenty-seven years in prison, walked out, and became president of the country that had jailed him, without taking revenge on yesterday's jailers.

The borders of the new states almost everywhere stayed colonial — drawn in European offices with no regard for who lived where. A great many of the conflicts of the following half-century grew out of exactly those lines.

## 1991 and after

The **Soviet Union** ceased to exist in 1991, and the Cold War ended with it. For a short while it seemed that history from here would be simple.

It was not. The internet and mobile phones arrived, connecting people more densely than ever. More people rose out of poverty in thirty years than in all previous history, chiefly thanks to China and India. And at the same time war returned to Europe, while climate change and new epidemics turned out to be problems no country solves alone, however strong it is.

**Why this matters today.** Of the twelve eras on this path, eleven have ended. The twelfth is the one we live in, and its outcome is not yet written. The one thing history offers is the understanding that the present arrangement of the world is neither permanent nor the only one possible. The people of 1914 did not think so either.
