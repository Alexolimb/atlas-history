The **Viking Age** is counted from 793, the raid on the monastery of Lindisfarne off the English coast, and ends in 1066. Less than three centuries, in which a few tens of thousands of people from Scandinavia redrew the map of Europe.

## A ship that changed the rules

The secret was the vessel. The longship drew little water, ran under both sail and oars, could enter any river and, at need, be dragged overland between them.

That meant distance from the sea was no protection. Rich monasteries and towns stood along rivers, and a force that could be raised in a week arrived by water without warning.

The picture of a savage in a horned helmet is wrong twice over. Vikings had no horned helmets; nineteenth-century artists invented them. And raiding was only part of the business: the same people carried furs, amber, walrus ivory and slaves, traded in Baghdad and Constantinople, founded towns and settled as farmers wherever the land was better than at home.

## How far they went

Danes and Norwegians settled England and Ireland; Dublin was founded by them. One band received land at the mouth of the Seine from the French king, and their descendants became the Normans, who conquered England a century and a half later. Vikings reached Spain and the Mediterranean and settled Iceland and Greenland.

Around the year 1000 Leif Erikson landed on the coast of North America. This is not legend: at L'Anse aux Meadows in Newfoundland a Norse settlement with a smithy and dwellings has been excavated. America was reached five hundred years before Columbus — but they could not hold on and left within a few years.

## The route from the Varangians to the Greeks

The Swedes went east rather than west, along the rivers of eastern Europe: the Volkhov, the Dnieper, the Volga. Their route linked the Baltic to Constantinople and to the Caspian, and towns grew along it: Ladoga, Novgorod, Kyiv.

**Kievan Rus** existed from 882 to 1240. The chronicle names incoming Varangians as its founders, but within two or three generations their names and language are Slavic: the newcomers dissolved into the locals rather than the other way round.

What follows is an ordinary state history for the period: tribute collection, expeditions against Constantinople, trade treaties. The turning point was 988, when Prince Volodymyr accepted Christianity from Byzantium. With the faith came writing in Cyrillic, building in stone, books and a link to the Christian world. Choosing eastern rather than western Christianity set a cultural boundary still visible on the map of Europe.

Rus was not a single state but a union of principalities under the seniority of Kyiv, and after the eleventh century it increasingly fought itself. In 1240 the Mongols took Kyiv, and the period ended.

## How the Viking Age ended

Scandinavia adopted Christianity, scattered jarls turned into kings of Denmark, Norway and Sweden, and a king finds it more profitable to collect taxes than to let his retinues go raiding the neighbours. The Vikings were not exterminated; they became Europeans.

**Why this matters today.** The same people appear in different sources as monsters, as merchants and as founders of states, depending on who was writing. The monks whose monasteries burned gave us the word "Viking" with all its blood; Arab travellers and Rus chronicles describe the same people differently. History is worth reading with a memory of who was holding the pen.
