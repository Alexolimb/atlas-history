The **American Civil War** ran from 1861 to 1865. Of all the wars in American history it took more lives than the rest put together.

## What the fighting was about

The dispute was about slavery. Attempts to explain the war by "states' rights" do not survive contact with the documents: in their declarations of secession the southern states named the defence of slaveholding as the reason themselves.

Economically north and south had diverged over half a century. The north built factories, railways and cities and absorbed millions of migrants from Europe. The south lived on exporting cotton, which on cheap enslaved labour brought enormous money; cotton was the leading American export.

The decisive question was the new land in the west. Would slavery exist in the new states? The majority in the Senate depended on it, and so did the future of the whole country. Every compromise held for a few years and broke.

## Lincoln

**Abraham Lincoln** was born in 1809 and died in 1865. He grew up in a log cabin, was largely self-taught, and before the presidency was a provincial lawyer.

His election in 1860 was the signal for the south. Lincoln did not promise to abolish slavery where it existed; he promised not to let it spread further west. That was enough: eleven states declared secession and formed the Confederacy.

The war turned out long and unprecedentedly bloody. Mass armies met carrying rifled weapons while using tactics designed for smoothbore muskets: formations advanced against positions that could now be swept four times further out. Railways, the telegraph, ironclads, trenches — this war resembles the First World War more than it does Napoleon's battles.

In 1863 Lincoln proclaimed the emancipation of the enslaved in the rebel states. The measure was partly military: it stripped the south of labour and opened the army to Black soldiers, about two hundred thousand of whom ultimately fought for the north. But it also changed the meaning of the war: from that point it was a war about abolishing slavery.

The south capitulated in the spring of 1865. A few days later Lincoln was shot in a theatre.

## What came of it

Slavery was abolished across the country by a constitutional amendment. About four million people became free.

What followed went worse than expected. Reconstruction, when federal troops secured the rights of the formerly enslaved in the south, lasted about ten years and ended in a political bargain. The southern states then passed laws that did not formally restore slavery but stripped Black citizens of the vote, the courts, schools and freedom of movement. That system lasted another hundred years, until the civil rights movement of the 1950s and 1960s.

**Why this matters today.** The abolition of slavery is not an event but a century-long process: the law passed in 1865 and began genuinely working in the 1960s. A written right becomes an operating one not at the moment of signature but when someone manages to force its enforcement.
